06 · How the final pixel gets assembled

Shading is just adding up a few terms. Each term is a small formula using the surface normal N, the light direction L, the view direction V, and the half-vector H = normalize(L+V). Toggle terms, drag sliders, and switch View mode to see each term in isolation.

final = ambient
  + diffuse · (N·L)
  + specular · (N·H)^shininess
  + fresnel · (1 − N·V)^p
View mode:

Ambient flat fill light

Light bouncing from everywhere. Keeps shadows from being pure black.

Diffuse ·(N·L)

Matte brightness = how directly the surface faces the light. N·L is 1 head-on, 0 at grazing. This is what makes a sphere look round.

Specular ·(N·H)^shininess

The shiny highlight. High shininess = tiny sharp dot (glossy); low = broad soft sheen (rough). This is the knob "roughness" controls.

Fresnel rim ·(1−N·V)^p

Edges facing away from you brighten (grazing reflection). Traces the silhouette — same trick as lesson 04's Fresnel driver.
